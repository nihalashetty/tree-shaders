The Life
of a Tree

From a single seed to a century of seasons. Scroll slowly.

I

Seed

A seed falls and settles into the soil. Everything the tree will become is folded inside it.

II

Sprout

The coat splits. A root reaches down, and two seed-leaves open toward the light.

III

Sapling

Each spring adds a little height. Each year, a thin new ring of wood.

IV

Branching

Limbs divide, and divide again — every fork a wager on where the light will be.

V

Canopy

Decades on, its leaves make a roof of shade. Birds raise their young in it; children swing from it.

VI

Midsummer

On the longest evenings, the air beneath the branches fills with fireflies.

VII

Autumn

The green drains away to ochre and rust, and the flocks turn south.

VIII

Letting go

One by one the leaves let go, while a squirrel stores away what the tree has given.

IX

Winter

Bare, the tree shows its architecture — a century of growth, written in branches.

X

Snow

Then the first snow settles along every limb. A cardinal keeps watch; someone builds a snowman.

XI

Thaw

The days lengthen. Snow slips from the branches, and the drifts shrink back into the earth.

XII

Blossom

Before a single leaf returns, the bare wood breaks into flower — and someone who once swung here comes back to sit beneath it.

XIII

Petal fall

Within days the petals let go, drifting down like slow snow, as the first leaves of a new year unfold.
